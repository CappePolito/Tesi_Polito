\begin{abstract}
	
	Industrial robotic systems increasingly rely on vision sensors to perform
	tasks requiring accurate spatial perception, such as object localization,
	inspection, and vision-guided manipulation. In these applications, the
	accuracy of the overall system strongly depends on the quality of the
	calibration process used to establish the geometric relationship between the
	camera and the robot reference frames.
	
	This thesis presents the design, implementation, and experimental validation
	of a complete vision-based calibration framework for industrial robotic
	applications. The proposed pipeline integrates camera intrinsic calibration,
	camera pose estimation using ChArUco calibration boards, and hand-eye
	calibration within a unified software architecture developed in Python using
	the OpenCV library.
	
	The experimental evaluation was conducted using a FANUC LR Mate 200iD robot
	equipped with a Cognex industrial camera operating in an eye-in-hand
	configuration. Intrinsic calibration was performed using ChArUco targets and
	validated through reprojection error analysis. Despite the presence of
	significant lens distortion, the calibration procedure achieved stable camera
	parameter estimation and sub-pixel reprojection accuracy. Multiple hand-eye
	calibration algorithms, including the Tsai, Park, Horaud, Andreff, and
	Daniilidis methods, were implemented and compared under identical acquisition
	conditions. The results demonstrated sub-millimetre cross-image consistency
	for all evaluated methods and highlighted the importance of dataset quality,
	motion diversity, and acquisition strategy in determining calibration
	accuracy.
	
	Additional investigations were performed to analyse convergence behaviour,
	dataset size requirements, and the influence of practical sources of error,
	including planar target limitations, motion degeneracy, image noise, and
	systematic target misalignment. The study showed that calibration performance
	is often more strongly influenced by the quality of the acquired data than by
	the specific hand-eye calibration algorithm employed.
	
	Beyond the laboratory validation, the knowledge and methodologies developed
	during this work were also applied to the implementation of a complete
	eye-to-hand calibration module for an industrial vision-guided robotic system
	deployed on an EPF SUPATA automatic feeding machine. The developed solution
	was integrated into a production environment and successfully validated
	through extended operation under real manufacturing conditions.
	
	The obtained results confirm the effectiveness of vision-based calibration
	techniques for industrial robotic systems and provide practical guidelines
	for the development, evaluation, and deployment of robust calibration
	pipelines in real-world applications.
	
\end{abstract}
